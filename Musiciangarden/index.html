Lessons

Every student grows differently

Lessons at Musician Garden are built around the student, not the syllabus. Chris takes time to learn each young musician’s personality, interests, and way of hearing — and shapes lessons to what is already there, helping it grow.

Guitar

Acoustic and electric, from first chords to confident playing. Good technique, careful practice, and music that means something to the student.

Ukulele

A wonderfully approachable first instrument — perfect for younger students building early confidence, rhythm, and the joy of making music.

Piano

Reading, theory, and expressiveness at the keys — foundations that serve a student for a lifetime, whatever instrument they later choose.

Lessons build confidence, creativity, character, discipline, musical understanding, and enjoyment. Technical skills are important — but they are tools that help students express something meaningful.

What we believe

Creativity Is a Gift. Growth Is Something We Tend.

Every student brings something unique to music — their personality, interests, experiences, ideas, and their own way of hearing and expressing what they feel.

Lessons at Musician Garden help students discover what is already within them while developing the skills, patience, discipline, and steady habits needed to help it grow.

Musical progress rarely comes through shortcuts or one dramatic breakthrough. More often, it grows little by little as a student keeps showing up, practices thoughtfully, and learns to care for both the gift and the craft.

Small, steady habits shape meaningful growth over time.

Teaching philosophy

Tending the Gift

I believe creativity is a gift, and caring for it is an act of stewardship.

That means encouraging the musical ideas and abilities already emerging within a student, while also helping them develop the patience, discipline, understanding, and character needed to tend those gifts well.

Musical growth rarely comes through shortcuts. It is cultivated through careful listening, thoughtful practice, repetition, perseverance, and respect for the craft.

A student’s gifts should be recognized and encouraged — but they should also be tended. Small, steady habits shape meaningful growth over time.

My goal is to help students enjoy music and express themselves creatively while also learning how to show up consistently, work through difficulty, care for their instruments, and take responsibility for their own growth.

About Chris

The teacher behind the garden

As a musician and teacher, I have learned that progress does not usually come from one dramatic breakthrough. It grows through showing up consistently, paying attention, working patiently, and learning to value the process.

That is part of what Musician Garden means to me. I want to recognize and encourage the God-given creativity within a student while also helping that student develop the habits, skills, and character needed to tend it faithfully.
